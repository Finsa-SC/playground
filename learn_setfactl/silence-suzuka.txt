---

1. GENERAL INFORMATION

Full Name: Silence Suzuka
Japanese Name: サイレンススズカ
Born: May 1, 1994
Died: November 1, 1998 (aged 4)
Country of Origin: Japan
Sex: Male (Stallion)
Color: Chestnut
Nickname: "Dimensional Fugitive" (Ijigen no Tōbōsha)
Reason for Nickname: Extraordinary front-running speed and ability to leave rivals far behind within moments.

---

2. PEDIGREE AND BREEDING

Sire: Sunday Silence (USA) – one of the most influential stallions in Japanese racing history.
Dam: Wakia (USA) – by Miswaki.
Breeder: Inahara Bokujo.
Owner: Keiji Nagai.
Trainer: Mitsuru Hashida.

Silence Suzuka was a direct product of the Sunday Silence bloodline, which dominated Japanese racing in the 1990s and 2000s. His dam side contributed speed and early maturity.

Total Starts: 16

4. THREE-YEAR-OLD SEASON (1997)

Debut: February 1, 1997 at Kyoto Racecourse – won a newcomer race by 7 lengths.

Key Races and Results:
- Yayoi Sho (G2): Finished 8th due to gate trouble.
- Tokyo Yushun (Japanese Derby, G1): Finished 9th.
- Hong Kong International Cup (December 1997): Finished 5th. This race marked the first partnership with jockey Yutaka Take. After this race, the stable decided to fully commit to an aggressive front-running tactic.

---

5. FOUR-YEAR-OLD SEASON (1998) – THE BREAKOUT YEAR

This was the season that defined Silence Suzuka. He won five consecutive graded stakes races and delivered record-breaking performances.

Race List (1998):
1. Valentine Stakes – 1st place.
2. Nakayama Kinen (G2) – 1st place (first graded stakes victory).
3. Kokura Daishoten (G3) – 1st place with a course record time of 1:46.5.
4. Kinko Sho (G2) – 1st place by 11 lengths, record time 1:57.8.
5. Takarazuka Kinen (G1) – 1st place. Defeated Stay Gold. This was his only Grade I victory.
6. Mainichi Okan (G2) – 1st place. Beat El Condor Pasa by 2.5 lengths.

Signature Tactic:
Silence Suzuka would break sharply from the gate, set extremely fast early fractions, and sustain a punishing pace that no rival could match. His front-running style was described as "unmatched in modern Japanese racing."

---

6. THE AUTUMN TENNO SHO – TRAGEDY AND FATAL INJURY

Date: November 1, 1998
Racecourse: Tokyo Racecourse
Race: Autumn Tenno Sho (G1)
Pre-Race Status: Overwhelming favorite.

Race Summary:
Silence Suzuka went to the front as expected. He recorded a blistering 57.4-second split for the first 1000 meters and opened a lead of approximately 15 lengths.

Injury:
Near the fourth turn, he suddenly slowed and veered off the track. He suffered a comminuted fracture of his left carpal bone (wrist area).

Outcome:
The injury was determined to be irreparable. On the same day, Silence Suzuka was euthanized at age 4.

Posthumous Recognition:
He received the JRA Special Award in recognition of his outstanding 1998 campaign.

Cultural Impact:
The event became known in Japan as "Silent Sunday" (Chinmoku no Nichiyōbi) due to the shocking loss of such a beloved horse.

---

7. LEGACY AND CULTURAL INFLUENCE

- Racing Legacy: Silence Suzuka's extreme front-running style influenced later racehorses such as Makros and Panthalassa.
- Media Presence: He is a major character in the multimedia franchise "Uma Musume: Pretty Derby." In the franchise, he is portrayed as a quiet, graceful, and introverted girl who loves running. Unlike real life, the anime depicts her surviving the Tenno Sho injury and recovering – a fictionalized act of wish-fulfillment for fans.
- Fan Memory: He is remembered as one of the most thrilling and tragic figures in Japanese horse racing – a star who burned brilliantly for one season before being lost too soon.

"Dimensional Fugitive" – a horse that is rare to see once in a few decades.
